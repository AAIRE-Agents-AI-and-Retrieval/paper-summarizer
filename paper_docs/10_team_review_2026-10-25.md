# Team review of 2026-10-25: points raised and what the data says

Three reviewers raised fifteen points (coverage of the training set, imitation versus understanding,
circularity of the evaluation, tokenizer confound, ROUGE versus semantics, independent evaluation
sets, and a follow-on agent/wiki idea). This file answers each from artifacts, marks what is not
yet measured, and costs the next step. New data: `data/thread_analysis.json`, `data/style_floor.json`,
`samples/train_samples.md` (generated locally, not distributed); scripts `scripts/thread_analysis.py`, `scripts/style_floor.py`,
`scripts/export_samples.py`.

Spearman correlation between an archive's training size and its test ROUGE-L: A −0.14, B −0.18,
C −0.20 (14 archives): the archives with the most training data are not scored better. By year the
students are flat (A 0.440–0.445 across four year bands). **By cohort they are not**: top-cited
test papers (n = 14,853) A 0.4436 / B 0.4696 / C 0.4794; random arXiv papers (n = 147) A 0.4025 /
B 0.4261 / C 0.4303, a drop of 0.04–0.05, larger than the sampling error (~0.016).

## How much of ROUGE-L is style, and how much is the paper

Each prediction scored against its own reference, and against a *different* paper's reference in the
same archive (shares schema, register and teacher phrasing, not the paper). 399 validation papers:

| | base | 1k papers | 5k | 20k | 70k |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| A matched | 0.327 | 0.416 | 0.424 | 0.435 | 0.450 |
| A other paper, same archive | 0.117 | 0.128 | 0.129 | 0.129 | 0.128 |
| A gap (paper-specific) | +0.211 | +0.288 | +0.295 | +0.306 | +0.321 |
| B gap | +0.275 | +0.323 | +0.332 | +0.332 | +0.346 |
| C gap (ex500 / 1k / 5k / 20k) | – | +0.325 / +0.332 | +0.340 | +0.353 | |

The teacher's own summaries score 0.123 against another paper's. The generic-style floor is about
0.12–0.13, reached at 1,000 papers and flat afterwards. The first 1,000 papers' gain is
**+0.089, of which +0.011 is the floor and +0.078 (88%) is paper-specific overlap**; the next
69,000 papers add +0.033, all paper-specific. Per field (A, 1k to 70k): tldr +0.032, problem +0.029,
method +0.032, results +0.034, limitations +0.032 — no field has saturated while others grow.
This is still lexical overlap with the teacher, and part of the first-1k step is length calibration
(length ratio 0.68 to 0.89); it cannot show that the content is correct.

## What was built in response (rows 9, 10 and 14 of the discussion)

An evaluation whose questions come from the paper, not from the teacher (`scisum/factcheck.py`),
run on 299 papers for the teacher, three students and two untrained models, and ACLSum as an
externally authored human reference. Results are in `05_results.md` and F7 in `06_findings.md`:

- Fact recall: teacher 0.618, B 0.572, C 0.574, A 0.527, untrained A 0.416. **B and C tie**
  (C − B = +0.002 [−0.013, +0.017]); both beat A by 0.045; distillation adds **+0.111** to the 4B model.
- The teacher answers wrongly 4.2% of the time, the students 4.7–5.7%: they trail by omission
  (not stated 23% vs 27–31%), which supports "students give up detail, not accuracy" on a measure
  that does not use the judge or the teacher as reference.
- Recall on *motivation* questions is 0.13–0.19 for every system including the teacher: the output
  schema has no field for why a paper matters or under what conditions its results hold.
- ACLSum separates only the best students from the untrained model (C − A +0.032 [+0.002, +0.063]).

Still open from the discussion: human verification of the gold answers (a 40-paper file is ready in
the local file written by `scisum factcheck export`), the retrieval-based evaluation (row 12; the endpoint offers an
embedding model and a reranker), the single-domain experiment (row 2), and the agent/wiki idea
(row 15).

**Also from the review**: the output schema has no field for *why a paper matters* or *under what
conditions its results hold*: recall on those question types is 0.13–0.19 and 0.40–0.48 for every
system including the teacher. Adding a field would be a schema change and therefore a new dataset.
